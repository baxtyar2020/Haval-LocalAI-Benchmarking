Final hardware-to-LLM fit report requirements.

A complete specification for reporting which local LLM sizes and models best fit a tested hardware configuration—and which model is the strongest match for Consumer, Gaming, and Commercial customers.

Baseline requirements · intended for implementation and future iteration
Primary product question
“What size and type of LLM can this hardware run well for the people who will actually use it?”

The report must answer practical suitability—not merely whether the model loads, which model has the most parameters, or which produces the highest quality score in isolation.

01 · Product principles

What the final report must accomplish.

The report is a decision instrument for hardware suitability, model deployment, and customer experience—not a generic leaderboard.

1

Start with the hardware

The tested system is the anchor. Every conclusion must describe the fit of a specific model configuration on that exact hardware.

2

Recommend a practical model envelope

Show fast/light, balanced sweet spot, high-capability, and stretch/out-of-range zones instead of naming only one winner.

3

Separate business recommendations

Consumer, Gaming, and Commercial must each have an independent best-match model. There must be no misleading universal winner.

4

Expose the human experience

Quality, first response, completion time, reliability, and hardware headroom must all contribute. A fast wrong answer or brilliant unusably slow answer cannot win.

MustParameter count alone is never sufficient. The report must also show dense versus Mixture-of-Experts architecture, total parameters, active parameters, quantization, actual model bytes, runtime memory, context/KV-cache headroom, response performance, reliability, and quality.

03 · Hardware capacity model

Translate measurements into an LLM operating envelope.

Ranges are derived from the tested configurations and their measured behavior; they are not universal parameter-size promises.

Fast / light
7–14B

Large headroom

Fast interaction and concurrency. Appropriate when responsiveness matters more than maximum reasoning depth.

Balanced sweet spot
26–30B

Best broad fit

Strong quality, comfortable memory margin, and natural response behavior across many personas.

High capability
48–80B

Practical tradeoffs

Higher quality may justify slower completion; MoE can behave differently from a dense model of similar total size.

Stretch / out of range
120B+

Uncomfortable fit

Loading is not enough. Low headroom, severe offload, instability, or unacceptable response time makes the configuration unsuitable.

MustThe capacity section must state that the displayed ranges describe the tested hardware and tested quantizations. It must not imply that every model with the same nominal parameter count performs identically.

05 · Workload intensity

Every persona is tested three ways.

Intensity is based on task complexity, output length, context, reasoning depth, and expected iteration—not simply prompt word count.

L

Light

Simple, short, low-context requests. The experience should feel immediate and require minimal reasoning or transformation.

Typical expectation: first response around 1–2 seconds; complete answer commonly within 10–30 seconds.
B

Balanced

Moderate context, several instructions, comparison or multi-step work. This represents the most common meaningful daily use.

Typical expectation: first response around 2–4 seconds; completion commonly within 30–120 seconds.
H

Heavy

Long context, complex reasoning, substantial coding or structured output. Customers accept more time only when the task justifies it.

Typical expectation: first response around 3–8 seconds; completion may range from 2–10 minutes by scenario.
MustEach scenario stores its own acceptable TTFT and total-completion target. The ranges above are defaults for design guidance, not universal pass/fail thresholds. The calculation uses the scenario-specific target ratio.
06 · Quality package

Measure only what matters for the scenario.

Not every persona needs math, coding, JSON, or creative writing. Applicable dimensions are normalized so unused dimensions do not punish a model.

A

Accuracy

Factual correctness, faithful extraction, grounded claims, and absence of material hallucination.

R

Reasoning

Sound analysis, correct relationships, defensible conclusions, and appropriate explanation depth.

M

Math

Correct calculations, units, formulas, intermediate logic, and numerical consistency.

P

Coding & Problem Solving

Correct, runnable, maintainable solutions; debugging quality; technical completeness.

I

Instruction Following

Compliance with constraints, requested tone, scope, length, ordering, and exclusions.

S

Structured Output / JSON

Valid schema, required fields, correct types, stable formatting, and parseable output.

ShouldCreative-writing quality is an additional applicable dimension for Writer & Communicator, Creative Prosumer, Progressive Creator, and Sales & Marketing scenarios. The prompt definition must state its rubric, such as originality, coherence, tone, audience fit, and style consistency.
07 · Calculation model

From one prompt to a business recommendation.

Calculations use exact values and round only for display. Labels and winners are selected from unrounded results.

1ScenarioPrompt, targets, applicable quality dimensions
2Workload fitQuality × experience × reliability
3Persona fitLight + Balanced + Heavy
4Business fitWeighted personas with coverage gates
5RecommendationBest qualified model within each business
Complete scoring chain

Quality leads. Experience and reliability keep the result honest.

Time ratior = actual response time / acceptable response time
Time normalizationg(r) = 100 if r ≤ 1; otherwise 100 × 2−(r−1)
Light experienceEL = .45g(TTFT ratio) + .55g(total-time ratio)
Balanced experienceEB = .35g(TTFT ratio) + .65g(total-time ratio)
Heavy experienceEH = .25g(TTFT ratio) + .75g(total-time ratio)

Persona and business scoring

Answers “how well does this model serve this type of customer?” It uses each persona’s workload scenarios and business-specific persona weights.

Hardware suitability scoring

Answers “how healthy is this model-and-hardware pairing overall?” It combines model quality, customer response experience, and hardware fit. It does not replace the business scores.

08 · Categories and gates

Clear labels with hard safety rails.

Categories are calculated from unrounded values. A displayed score rounded to 90 may remain Strong Match if the exact score is below 90.

Numeric rangeCategoryMeaningRecommended interpretation
90–100Excellent MatchStrong quality, natural experience, stable fitPreferred configuration for the evaluated scope
80–89.9Strong MatchDependable fit with manageable tradeoffsRecommended for normal use
70–79.9Acceptable MatchUsable, but limitations are visibleUse when tradeoffs are understood
60–69.9Marginal MatchRuns, but efficiency or experience is weakLimited, occasional, or demonstration use
Below 60Not RecommendedQuality, experience, or hardware fit is insufficientChoose a smaller/different model or stronger hardware
Non-numericFailedModel or scenario did not completeShow the reason; never create substitute scores

Global hardware suitability gates

  • Missing, OOM, timeout, load failure, or incomplete scenario → Failed.
  • Quality Q < 55 or Hardware Fit H < 40 → Not Recommended.
  • Severe swap or unstable execution caps the result at Marginal Match.
  • Representative customer task over 15 minutes caps the result at Marginal Match.
  • Quality Q < 65 caps the result at Acceptable Match.

Persona and business protection rules

  • Failed Heavy workloads cannot be hidden by Light performance.
  • Business aggregation must use numeric persona results, never label averages.
  • Minimum persona coverage is required before a business category is issued.
  • Critical persona failure may cap the business result.
  • A business winner must be selected only from qualified completed models.
09 · Final report information architecture

Required order and content.

The design starts with the purchasing/deployment decision and progressively reveals the supporting evidence.

00
Executive hardware verdict
Answer the main question immediately.
Required
  • Dynamic title: Evaluation of <Product Name> against Different LLM Sizes.
  • Primary verdict: best general-purpose parameter range.
  • Secondary verdict: highest practical capability range and out-of-range boundary.
  • Disclosure: measured or hypothetical status.
01
Tested hardware configuration
Establish exactly what system the conclusions apply to.
Required
  • Four desktop tiles: System, GPU + VRAM, CPU, System Memory.
  • Use device-aware inline SVG: tower, laptop, mini PC, GPU, CPU, DIMM.
  • Do not show OS or Power Mode tiles in the final customer report.
  • State why model bytes, KV cache, overhead, and headroom matter.
01A
Realistic customer scenario
Anchor model speed in a task customers can understand.
Required
  • Use the approved scenario: a user provides a medium-length 10-page report and asks for a detailed, well-structured answer that summarizes and simplifies it and identifies key takeaways.
  • Show the visual sequence: 10-page content → AI model → key insights.
  • Use document, AI-chip, and lightbulb inline SVG icons.
  • Every completed model must show its estimated completion time for this same scenario.
02
LLM capacity envelope
Show the useful range—not only what loads.
Required
  • Fast/light, balanced sweet spot, high-capability, stretch/out-of-range zones.
  • Associate tested models with their zone.
  • Explain dense versus MoE differences.
  • Base zones on tested quantization and measured experience.
03
Best model by business
Provide three independent deployment recommendations.
Required
  • One Consumer winner card in teal.
  • One Gaming winner card in purple.
  • One Commercial winner card in blue.
  • No “recommended overall model” card.
04
Tested-model evidence table
Show why each model occupies its capacity zone.
Required
  • Model, architecture, total/active parameters, quantization, bytes.
  • Memory used, generation speed, TTFT, customer task time.
  • Hardware zone and calculated suitability category.
  • Failed models remain visible with em dashes and failure reason.
04A
Final Verdict response-time table
Make realistic task completion directly comparable.
Required
  • Columns: model, generation speed, TTFT, estimated seconds, min:sec, and suitability category.
  • Use the same approved 10-page customer scenario for every model.
  • Calculate from exact TTFT and generation-speed inputs; round only the displayed result.
  • Do not expose the internal token-count estimation formula in the report.
05
Business match matrix
Compare all models across Consumer, Gaming, and Commercial.
Required
  • One row per tested or expected model.
  • Three final business match columns.
  • Parameter configuration and recommended use.
  • No single overall score, rank, or winner column.
06
Selected sweet-spot drill-down
Explain the most broadly practical model configuration.
Recommended
  • Model identity, architecture, total/active parameter boxes, quantization.
  • Quality Q, Experience E, Hardware Fit H, and reliability.
  • Global suitability score and category.
  • Model footprint, observed memory, and headroom.
07
Persona workload tables for every model
Reveal exactly which customers and workload intensities each model fits.
Required
  • Repeat one complete table for every tested model.
  • Model header: name, architecture, total/active parameters, quantization, profile.
  • Three-business summary strip above each model table.
  • Rows: Business, Persona, Light, Balanced, Heavy, Overall, Final Match.
  • All 20 personas appear in the same order for every model.
  • Each table contains 60 workload-level results and 20 persona results.
  • Use Excellent, Strong, Acceptable, Marginal, Not Recommended, or Failed.
  • If a workload failed, display an em dash and the evidence-backed reason.
08
Scoring equation card
Make the calculation transparent without overwhelming the decision story.
Required
  • Dark premium card with concise equations.
  • Show quality, timing, experience, reliability, workload, persona, business, and hardware formulas.
  • Do not display the internal 8,000-token task-time formula in the customer report.
  • State that exact values drive categories.
09
Persona reference table
Make the score meanings understandable to non-benchmark readers.
Required
  • Business, persona, typical day-to-day work, and general AI need.
  • Use consistent business colors.
  • Clarify that personas are task-focused rather than demographic.
  • Keep descriptions brief and practical.

11 · Prompt and scenario traceability

Every score must be explainable.

The final customer report may remain concise, but the application must retain the prompt, rubric, targets, raw response, and evaluator evidence behind every cell.

Scenario record requirements

  • Unique scenario ID and version.
  • Business and persona.
  • Light, Balanced, or Heavy intensity.
  • Complete actual prompt sent to the model.
  • Input context or attachment reference.
  • Applicable quality dimensions and weights.
  • Expected answer requirements and evaluator rubric.
  • Acceptable TTFT and total-response target.
  • Number of attempts and success criteria.
  • Raw timings, output, errors, and evaluation evidence.

12 · Visual and interaction requirements

Elegant, calm, and immediately understandable.

The design should feel premium and editorial—closer to an Apple-style product report than a dense engineering console.

Visual language

  • Georgia/Fraunces-like serif headlines with clean sans-serif body text.
  • Rounded cards, restrained shadows, generous whitespace.
  • Consumer teal, Gaming purple, Commercial blue throughout.
  • Category color appears in pills and indicators, not overwhelming full backgrounds.
  • Inline SVG icons only; report remains self-contained and offline.

13 · Final acceptance checklist

Definition of done.

A report is complete only when the conclusions, evidence, calculations, and presentation agree with one another.

Data and calculation

  • Every expected non-excluded model appears exactly once in comparison sections.
  • Every tested model has its own Section 07 persona table or failure inset.
  • All 20 personas appear in consistent order for every completed model.
  • Light, Balanced, Heavy, Overall, and Final Match are present.
  • Quality dimensions are scenario-appropriate and normalized.
  • Timing uses scenario-specific acceptable targets.
  • Exact unrounded inputs determine labels and winners.
  • Failure, quality, stability, timing, and coverage gates are applied.
  • Consumer, Gaming, and Commercial winners come from their own business scores.
  • No universal best-model recommendation appears.

Report and presentation

  • The hardware verdict and model-size envelope appear before model detail.
  • Dense/MoE, total/active parameters, quantization, and bytes are visible.
  • Hardware headroom is distinguished from “model loaded.”
  • Business colors remain consistent across cards and tables.
  • Categories use the approved six-state taxonomy.
  • Failed values use em dashes and an evidence-backed reason.
  • Tables remain usable on smaller displays.
  • The report prints cleanly and works fully offline.
  • Persona definitions explain the customer groups briefly.
  • Measured versus hypothetical status is explicit.
Final principleThe strongest report is not the one with the most scores. It is the one that lets a customer look at a hardware configuration and confidently understand which LLM sizes fit, which exact model fits each business, where the experience begins to degrade, and why.
